Pet CBD is one of the fastest-growing parts of the hemp market: oils, chews and treats sold for dogs and cats. If you're thinking about it, here's what to know first.

## Start with your vet

The FDA has not approved any CBD product for animals, and research in dogs is still limited. Veterinarians can't always prescribe or recommend CBD, depending on state rules, but they can discuss it. They also know your dog's health and medications. CBD can interact with drugs your dog takes, so this conversation matters.

## Two ingredients that are dangerous for dogs

- **THC.** Dogs are much more sensitive to THC than people. THC poisoning in dogs can cause wobbliness, dribbling urine, a slow heart rate and extreme lethargy. Choose **THC-free (isolate or broad-spectrum)** pet products and confirm it on the lab report.
- **Xylitol.** This sweetener appears in some human gummies and is **toxic to dogs**, even in small amounts. Never give your dog CBD products made for people.

If your dog eats a human cannabis edible, call your vet or an animal poison control line right away.

## Choosing a pet CBD product

1. **Made for pets.** Pet products are dosed for smaller bodies and use dog-safe flavors and ingredients.
2. **Batch-specific COA** showing CBD content, **THC as ND (not detected)**, and contaminant testing ([how to read a COA](/guides/how-to-read-a-cbd-coa/)).
3. **Clear mg per serving.** Treats should list mg per chew; oils should list mg per mL.
4. **Serving guidance by weight.** Reputable brands give servings by body weight. Start at the low end.
5. **No disease claims.** Products claiming to treat arthritis, seizures or anxiety are making claims no hemp product is approved for.

## Possible side effects

Effects reported in dogs include:

- sleepiness or lethargy
- loose stool or upset stomach
- changes in appetite
- increased liver enzymes (a vet can monitor this with bloodwork if your dog takes CBD regularly)

Stop giving it and call your vet if you notice anything unusual.

## Cats and other pets

Cats process many substances differently from dogs, and there's even less research on CBD in cats. Use only products made for cats, and only after talking to your vet.
