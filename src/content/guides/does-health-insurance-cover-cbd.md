**Short answer: no, with one exception.** Health insurance plans, including Medicare and Medicaid, generally don't cover the CBD oils, gummies and creams sold in stores and online. Here's why, and what you can do about the cost.

## Why insurance doesn't cover CBD products

Insurers cover drugs that are approved by the FDA and prescribed by a doctor. Hemp-derived CBD products are neither:

- They're sold as consumer products, **not FDA-approved drugs**.
- The FDA has not approved CBD as a dietary supplement either.
- Product quality and strength vary from brand to brand, so there's no standard product for a plan to cover.

## The exception: prescription CBD

There is one FDA-approved prescription drug containing CBD, **Epidiolex**. It's approved for specific seizure conditions. Because it's a prescription drug, it can be covered by insurance, usually with prior authorization, like other specialty medications. It's prescribed by a doctor and isn't the same as over-the-counter CBD.

## What about HSAs and FSAs?

Health savings and flexible spending accounts generally only cover qualified medical expenses. Over-the-counter CBD usually isn't one. Rules depend on your plan, so check with your plan administrator before using account funds. Don't rely on a store's claim that a product is "HSA eligible."

## Ways to pay less for CBD

Since you'll most likely be paying out of pocket:

1. **Compare on cost per mg of CBD**, not price per bottle. Divide the price by total mg of CBD.
2. **Buy bigger sizes** when you know you like a product. The cost per mg usually drops.
3. **Use subscriptions and loyalty programs.** Many brands take 15–25% off repeat orders.
4. **Watch for sales.** Most CBD brands run regular promotions. That's what [Daily Green Deals](/deals/) tracks.
5. **Check assistance programs.** Some brands offer discounts for veterans, seniors, people with disabilities or low-income customers.
6. **Don't sacrifice quality for price.** A cheap product with no lab report isn't a deal. See [how to read a COA](/guides/how-to-read-a-cbd-coa/).

## Talk to your doctor

If you're considering CBD alongside a medical condition or prescription medications, talk to your doctor or pharmacist. CBD can interact with some drugs, and your doctor can tell you whether any covered treatments apply to your situation.
