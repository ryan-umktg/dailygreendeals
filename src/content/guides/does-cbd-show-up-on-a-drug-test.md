Short answer: standard workplace drug tests don't look for CBD. They look for THC, more specifically a THC breakdown product called THC-COOH. CBD on its own doesn't trigger a positive result. People who use CBD do sometimes fail drug tests, though, and almost always for the same reason: **the product contained THC**.

## Why CBD users fail drug tests

**Full-spectrum products contain THC on purpose.** "Full spectrum" means the extract keeps the plant's other cannabinoids, including a small amount of THC. Taken daily, especially at higher servings, that THC can build up enough to be detected.

**Labels aren't always accurate.** Hemp products aren't reviewed by the FDA before sale. Independent testing has repeatedly found products whose contents don't match their labels. One widely cited study of CBD products bought online found THC in about one in five of them.

**Cross-contamination.** Products made on shared equipment can pick up trace THC even when the formula is meant to be THC-free.

## How to lower your risk

1. **Choose isolate or broad-spectrum products.** Isolate is CBD alone. Broad spectrum keeps other compounds but removes THC.
2. **Read the COA.** Total THC should show as "ND" (not detected) for the batch you're buying. Our [guide to reading a COA](/guides/how-to-read-a-cbd-coa/) walks through it.
3. **Buy from brands that publish full, batch-specific lab reports.** Make sure the batch number matches your package.
4. **Be careful with smokable hemp and "hemp flower" products.** These naturally contain THCA, which converts to THC when heated.

Even with all of that, no product can be guaranteed to keep you under a test's threshold. If you're subject to testing, the safest choice is to avoid cannabinoid products or ask your employer or program what's allowed.

## Who should be most careful

- People in federally regulated jobs (transportation, aviation, federal employment, many healthcare roles)
- Athletes covered by anti-doping rules (see [CBD for athletes](/guides/cbd-for-athletes/))
- Anyone on probation, parole or in a treatment program with testing

## What about the new federal rules?

From **December 11, 2026** (unless delayed again), finished hemp products are limited to **0.4 mg of total THC per container**. That should bring THC levels in legal products down a lot. It doesn't remove the risk, though. Labels can still be wrong, and products made under the old rules may still be for sale for a while.
