CBD is oil-soluble, not water-soluble. That's why most CBD comes dissolved in a carrier oil, and why so much of a swallowed dose doesn't reach the bloodstream. "Nano," "nanoemulsion," "liposomal" and "water-soluble" CBD are all ways of getting around that by breaking the oil into very small droplets that mix with water.

## The main types

- **Nanoemulsion:** CBD oil broken into tiny droplets (often under 100 nanometers) and held in water by emulsifiers. Common in CBD drinks, drink mixes and some tinctures.
- **Liposomal:** CBD wrapped in liposomes, which are tiny spheres made from fats like lecithin.
- **Water-soluble powders:** CBD bound to a carrier, such as a starch, so it dissolves in drinks.

## Do they work better?

The theory is sound: smaller droplets have more surface area and mix with the water-based environment of the gut, which can mean faster and more consistent absorption. Some small studies have found faster absorption and higher blood levels from emulsified CBD than from plain CBD oil.

Be skeptical of the bigger marketing claims, though. Claims like "100% absorbed" or "10x stronger" aren't backed by independent research. Results depend heavily on the specific formula. A water-soluble product also isn't automatically better than a regular oil taken with food, which raises absorption on its own.

## Is it worth paying more?

Nano products usually cost more per milligram. They make the most sense when:

- you want CBD in a **drink**, where regular oil just floats on top, or
- you'd prefer something you can **swallow without food**.

For everyday use, compare the **cost per mg of CBD** across formats ([our tincture guide](/guides/cbd-tinctures/) shows the math), and decide whether the convenience is worth the difference.

## What to check before buying

- **A batch-specific COA** showing the CBD content matches the label ([how to read one](/guides/how-to-read-a-cbd-coa/)). Emulsions are mostly water, so a 30 mL bottle can hold far less CBD than a regular oil of the same size.
- **The ingredient list.** Emulsifiers like polysorbate 80, gum arabic or sunflower lecithin are normal. Check for sweeteners or allergens.
- **THC content**, especially for full-spectrum products. From **December 11, 2026**, finished hemp products are capped at 0.4 mg total THC per container.

## Safety notes

The same cautions apply as for any CBD product. CBD can interact with prescription medications, so check with a doctor or pharmacist first if you take any. Hemp-derived CBD products are not FDA-approved, and their labels can't legally claim to treat any condition.
